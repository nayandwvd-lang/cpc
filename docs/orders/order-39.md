# Order XXXIX — Temporary Injunctions and Interlocutory Orders

Order XXXIX is where most civil litigation is actually decided. A suit for possession may take eleven years to reach judgment; the application under Rules 1 and 2 is heard in the first fortnight, and the order made on it usually determines who holds the property while the suit runs — which, for most litigants, is the only question that matters. The Order is short, its language is discretionary throughout, and almost the whole of its content is judge-made: the three golden principles appear nowhere in the text.

| Rule | One-line function |
|---|---|
| R. 1 | Temporary injunction to restrain waste, damage, alienation, wrongful sale in execution, or dispossession — property-focused |
| R. 2 | Injunction to restrain a breach of contract or other injury — conduct-focused |
| R. 2A | Consequence of disobedience: attachment of property and detention in civil prison |
| R. 3 | Notice to the opposite party before granting; the proviso governs ex parte injunctions |
| R. 3A | Where an ex parte injunction is granted, the application **shall** be disposed of within thirty days |
| R. 4 | Order for injunction may be discharged, varied or set aside |
| R. 5 | Injunction against a corporation binds its officers |
| RR. 6–10 | Interim sale, detention/preservation/inspection of property, deposit of money admitted to be due |

::: info The architecture in one breath
Rule 1 protects **property**; Rule 2 restrains **conduct**. Rule 3 is the price of speed — an ex parte order may be had, but only on recorded reasons and with immediate service. Rule 3A is the safeguard against the ex parte order that lives forever. Rule 2A is the enforcement mechanism, and the only provision in the Order with teeth. The three golden principles — prima facie case, balance of convenience, irreparable injury — are nowhere in the text; they are entirely the creation of the courts, and they are what the judge is actually applying.
:::

## Rules 1 and 2 — the grant of a temporary injunction

### 1. Verbatim Bare Act Text & Statutory Mechanics

> **Rule 1. Cases in which temporary injunction may be granted.** — Where in any suit it is proved by affidavit or otherwise —
> **(a)** that any property in dispute in a suit is in danger of being wasted, damaged or alienated by any party to the suit, or wrongfully sold in execution of a decree, or
> **(b)** that the defendant threatens, or intends, to remove or dispose of his property with a view to defrauding his creditors,
> **(c)** that the defendant threatens to dispossess the plaintiff or otherwise cause injury to the plaintiff in relation to any property in dispute in the suit,
>
> the Court may by order grant a temporary injunction to restrain such act, or make such other order for the purpose of staying and preventing the wasting, damaging, alienation, sale, removal or disposition of the property or dispossession of the plaintiff, or otherwise causing injury to the plaintiff in relation to any property in dispute in the suit as the Court thinks fit, until the disposal of the suit or until further orders.

> **Rule 2. Injunction to restrain repetition or continuance of breach.** — (1) In any suit for restraining the defendant from committing a breach of contract or other injury of any kind, whether compensation is claimed in the suit or not, the plaintiff may, at any time after the commencement of the suit, and either before or after judgment, apply to the Court for a temporary injunction to restrain the defendant from committing the breach of contract or injury complained of, or any breach of contract or injury of a like kind arising out of the same contract or relating to the same property or right.
>
> (2) The Court may by order grant such injunction, on such terms as to the duration of the injunction, keeping an account, giving security, or otherwise, as the Court thinks fit.

> **Rule 3. Before granting injunction, Court to direct notice to opposite party.** — The Court shall in all cases, except where it appears that the object of granting the injunction would be defeated by the delay, before granting an injunction, direct notice of the application for the same to be given to the opposite party:
>
> *Provided that, where it is proposed to grant an injunction without giving notice of the application to the opposite party, the Court shall record the reasons for its opinion that the object of granting the injunction would be defeated by delay, and require the applicant —*
> *(a) to deliver to the opposite party, or to send to him by registered post, immediately after the order granting the injunction has been made, a copy of the application for injunction together with — (i) a copy of the affidavit filed in support of the application; (ii) a copy of the plaint; and (iii) copies of documents on which the applicant relies, and*
> *(b) to file, on the day on which such injunction is granted or on the day immediately following that day, an affidavit stating that the copies aforesaid have been so delivered or sent.*

> **Rule 3A. Court to dispose of application for injunction within thirty days.** — Where an injunction has been granted without giving notice to the opposite party, the Court shall make an endeavour to finally dispose of the application within thirty days from the date on which the injunction was granted; and where it is unable so to do, it shall record its reasons for such inability.

> **Rule 4. Order for injunction may be discharged, varied or set aside.** — Any order for an injunction may be discharged, or varied, or set aside by the Court, on application made thereto by any party dissatisfied with such order:
>
> *Provided that if in an application for temporary injunction or in any affidavit supporting such application a party has knowingly made a false or misleading statement in relation to a material particular and the injunction was granted without giving notice to the opposite party, the Court shall vacate the injunction unless, for reasons to be recorded, it considers that it is not necessary so to do in the interests of justice:*
>
> *Provided further that where an order for injunction has been passed after giving to a party an opportunity of being heard, the order shall not be discharged, varied or set aside on the application of that party except where such discharge, variation or setting aside has been necessitated by a change in the circumstances, or unless the Court is satisfied that the order has caused undue hardship to that party.*

> **Rule 5. Injunction to corporation binding on its officers.** — An injunction directed to a corporation is binding not only on the corporation itself, but also on all members and officers of the corporation whose personal action it seeks to restrain.

**Statutory mechanics**

| Operative expression | Character | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| R. 1: "**may** by order grant" | Discretionary | There is no right to a temporary injunction. The court's discretion is judicial, to be exercised on settled principles — but it is discretion, and an appellate court will not interfere merely because it would have decided differently. |
| R. 1: "**property in dispute in a suit**" | Jurisdictional limitation | Rule 1(a) and 1(c) operate only on property **in dispute in the suit**. An injunction cannot be granted under Rule 1 to protect property that is not the subject-matter of the suit. Rule 1(b) is the narrow exception, addressed to disposal of the defendant's property to defraud creditors. |
| R. 1: "it is **proved** by affidavit or otherwise" | Evidentiary threshold | The applicant bears the burden. A bare application without an affidavit setting out the facts is incompetent. |
| R. 2(1): "**at any time after the commencement of the suit**, and either before or after judgment" | Temporal | Rule 2 relief presupposes a pending suit. There is no free-standing injunction application. |
| R. 2(2): "on such terms as to… **keeping an account, giving security**" | Enabling | The court may impose conditions. An injunction on terms — security, undertaking as to damages, deposit — is often the right order where the balance is fine. |
| R. 3: "The Court **shall in all cases**… direct notice" | Mandatory, subject to a single exception | Notice is the rule. Ex parte is the exception, available only "where it appears that the object of granting the injunction would be defeated by the delay". |
| R. 3 proviso: "**shall record the reasons**" | Mandatory; a condition of validity | An ex parte injunction granted without recorded reasons is granted in breach of a mandatory requirement and is liable to be set aside on that ground alone. |
| R. 3 proviso (a), (b): service **immediately**; affidavit of compliance on the same or next day | Mandatory | Non-compliance exposes the order to vacation. The compliance affidavit is a distinct obligation and is routinely overlooked. |
| R. 3A: "**shall make an endeavour**… within thirty days… **shall record its reasons**" | Directory in form; enforced in substance | An ex parte injunction not disposed of within thirty days, without recorded reasons, is an abuse. The remedy for the aggrieved party is to move for vacation and, if necessary, Article 227. |
| R. 4 first proviso: "the Court **shall vacate** the injunction" | **Mandatory**, subject to a recorded exception | Where the applicant has knowingly made a false or misleading statement on a material particular **and** the injunction was ex parte, vacation is mandatory unless the court records reasons why the interests of justice require otherwise. |
| R. 4 second proviso | Restrictive | A party who has been heard cannot re-agitate; he must show a **change in circumstances** or **undue hardship**. This forecloses serial applications. |
| R. 5 | Extension of the binding effect | An injunction against a company binds its directors and officers personally, so far as their personal action is sought to be restrained. This is what makes Rule 2A enforceable against individuals behind a corporate defendant. |

::: warning Section 41 of the Specific Relief Act sits over the whole Order
Order XXXIX supplies the procedure; the substantive bars are in **Section 41 of the Specific Relief Act, 1963**, which lists the cases in which an injunction cannot be granted at all — to restrain a person from prosecuting a pending judicial proceeding, to restrain proceedings in a court not subordinate to that from which the injunction is sought, to restrain a person from applying to a legislative body, to restrain proceedings in a criminal matter, to prevent a breach of contract which could not be specifically enforced, to prevent a continuing breach in which the applicant has acquiesced, where equally efficacious relief can be obtained by any other usual mode of proceeding, where the applicant's conduct disentitles him to assistance, or where the applicant has no personal interest. A well-run defence to an injunction application begins with Section 41, not with the balance of convenience.
:::

### 2. Plain-English Conceptual Demystification

**Sub-module A — the three golden principles.**

Nothing in Order XXXIX mentions them. They are the settled judicial gloss, and they are conjunctive: all three must be satisfied.

| Principle | What it actually requires | What it does not require |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Prima facie case** | A serious question to be tried; a case which, on the material presently available, is not frivolous or vexatious and discloses a real prospect of success. The court forms a tentative view. | It does **not** require the plaintiff to establish that he will succeed, nor to prove his case on a balance of probabilities. The court does not decide the suit at the interlocutory stage. |
| **2. Balance of convenience** | A comparison of the mischief: which party will suffer greater inconvenience if the order is wrongly made or wrongly refused? The court weighs comparative hardship and, importantly, the preservation of the status quo. | It is not a headcount of hardships, and it is not a measure of who is more sympathetic. |
| **3. Irreparable injury** | Injury which cannot be adequately compensated in money — because the subject-matter is unique (land, a work of art, goodwill, a going concern), because the defendant is not good for the money, or because the harm is not susceptible of measurement. | "Irreparable" does not mean "very large". A quantifiable money loss recoverable from a solvent defendant is, by definition, reparable. |

**The fourth, unwritten principle: conduct.** An injunction is equitable relief. Delay (laches), suppression of material facts, unclean hands, and acquiescence each defeat an application that would otherwise succeed on the three principles. Suppression in particular is fatal — see the first proviso to Rule 4.

**Sub-module B — Rule 1 and Rule 2 distinguished.**

| | **Rule 1** | **Rule 2** |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | **Property** in dispute in the suit | **Conduct** — breach of contract or "other injury of any kind" |
| Typical use | Restraining alienation, waste, dispossession, wrongful sale in execution | Restraining a breach of a negative covenant, infringement, defamation, disclosure of confidential information, invocation of a bank guarantee |
| Requires property in dispute? | Yes (except R. 1(b)) | No |
| Requires a claim for compensation? | No | Expressly no — "whether compensation is claimed in the suit or not" |
| Consequence of disobedience | Rule 2A | Rule 2A |

In practice almost every application is headed "under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 read with Section 151", and it is right that it should be — the two rules overlap, and Section 151 covers the residue where neither strictly applies.

**Sub-module C — the ex parte injunction, and the discipline of Rule 3.**

An ex parte injunction is an order made against a person who has not been heard. The Code permits it only because delay would sometimes defeat the relief entirely — the tree will be felled, the property sold, the confidential information published. But it attaches four conditions:

1. **The court must form the opinion** that the object of granting the injunction would be defeated by delay;
2. **The court must record its reasons** for that opinion. Not a formula; reasons referable to the facts;
3. **The applicant must serve** the application, the supporting affidavit, the plaint and the documents on the opposite party **immediately**;
4. **The applicant must file an affidavit of compliance** on the day of the order or the day after.

And Rule 3A adds a fifth: the application must be finally disposed of within **thirty days**, or the court must record why it could not be.

::: danger Where ex parte injunctions actually go wrong
The commonest defect is not the absence of urgency; it is the absence of **recorded reasons**. A court that writes "heard counsel; ad interim injunction as prayed" has not complied with the proviso to Rule 3. The second commonest is failure to file the compliance affidavit under proviso (b). Both are grounds for vacation independent of the merits, and both should be the first two paragraphs of any application under Rule 4.
:::

**Sub-module D — the duty of full and frank disclosure.**

Because the opposite party is absent, an applicant for an ex parte injunction owes the court a duty to disclose everything material — including facts that damage his own case. Suppression of a prior order, an earlier suit, a notice received, an adverse document, or the true state of possession, is fatal.

The first proviso to Rule 4 gives this teeth: where a party has **knowingly** made a false or misleading statement on a material particular and the injunction was granted ex parte, the court **shall vacate** it. The only escape is a recorded finding that the interests of justice do not require vacation.

**Sub-module E — status quo, and what it means.**

Courts frequently pass orders "to maintain status quo". The expression is a trap unless defined. Status quo as of **when** — the date of the suit, the date of the application, the date of the order? Status quo as to **what** — possession, title, construction, the state of the register?

An order that simply says "status quo to be maintained" invites an execution-stage dispute about what it meant. A well-drafted prayer, and a well-drafted order, says: *"status quo as regards possession and the nature of construction on the suit property as obtaining on the date of this order"*.

**Sub-module F — the categories in which an injunction is refused.**

| Category | Reason |
|---|---|
| Money claims | Damages are an adequate remedy; there is no irreparable injury. The correct application is Order XXXVIII Rule 5 (attachment before judgment), not Order XXXIX. |
| Contracts of personal service | Cannot be specifically enforced (s. 14, Specific Relief Act, 1963); therefore no injunction (s. 41(e)). |
| Restraining proceedings in a court | Section 41(a) and (b), Specific Relief Act, 1963. The remedy is Section 10 CPC (stay of suit) or a transfer application, not an injunction. |
| Unconditional bank guarantees | Restrained only on established **fraud of an egregious kind** going to the root of the transaction, or **irretrievable injustice**. Commercial disputes about the underlying contract are not enough. |
| Against a stranger to the suit | An injunction binds parties; a stranger must be impleaded under Order I Rule 10. |
| Where the plaintiff has acquiesced | Section 41(f) and (h); and, at the interlocutory stage, delay defeats equity. |
| To restrain the State from performing a statutory duty | Section 41 and the general principle that public functions are not lightly restrained. |

### 3. Courtroom & Practical Illustrations

**Illustration 1 — the construction case.** P owns a plot and alleges that D, the adjoining owner, is raising a wall encroaching two feet onto P's land. P files a suit for mandatory injunction and possession, with an application under Rules 1 and 2 for a restraint on further construction. Construction is at plinth level.

- **Sword (plaintiff):** Prima facie case established by the sale deed, the sanctioned plan and the demarcation report. Balance of convenience overwhelmingly favours restraint at plinth level: stopping now costs D a delay; allowing completion means P must later seek demolition. Irreparable injury — land is not compensable in damages, and the encroachment, once built upon, becomes a fait accompli. Ask specifically for an injunction restraining construction **beyond the existing plinth**, and for a Commissioner under Order XXVI Rule 9 to record the state of construction.
- **Shield (defendant):** Attack the demarcation. Contend that the plan is sanctioned, that P delayed until the investment was made (laches and acquiescence — P watched the foundation being laid for three months), and that if P ultimately succeeds, demolition is a perfectly available final remedy so the injury is not irreparable. Offer an undertaking that the construction will be at D's own risk and subject to the result of the suit — courts frequently accept this in preference to a stoppage order.

::: tip The Commissioner is the underrated application
In any construction, encroachment or possession dispute, an application under **Order XXVI Rule 9** for a Local Commissioner to inspect and report on the physical state of the property, filed on the same day as the injunction application, is worth more than three affidavits. It fixes the factual position on the date of institution and forecloses the "as it always was" defence.
:::

**Illustration 2 — restraint on alienation.** P sues for specific performance of an agreement to sell dated 2023, and seeks an injunction restraining D from alienating or creating third-party rights in the suit property.

- **Sword (plaintiff):** The property is in dispute in the suit and is in danger of being alienated — Rule 1(a) squarely. Irreparable injury is established by the nature of the relief: specific performance of an agreement relating to immovable property. Plead readiness and willingness in the plaint (s. 16(c), Specific Relief Act, 1963) — an injunction in a specific performance suit routinely fails because readiness and willingness are inadequately pleaded, which destroys the prima facie case.
- **Shield (defendant):** Contend that P has not pleaded or demonstrated readiness and willingness; that the agreement is unregistered and inadequately stamped; that time was of the essence and P failed to perform. On the balance of convenience, point to the doctrine of *lis pendens* under **s. 52 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882** — any transferee during the pendency takes subject to the result, so P is protected without an injunction. This is a genuinely strong argument and often succeeds in converting a restraint into a direction to disclose any transfer.

**Illustration 3 — the ex parte injunction obtained by suppression.** P obtains an ex parte injunction restraining D from interfering with P's possession of a godown. P does not disclose that six months earlier a criminal court had passed an order under s. 145 CrPC (now the corresponding provision of the BNSS) attaching the property and finding D in possession.

- **Sword (defendant):** Application under **Rule 4, first proviso**. The suppressed order goes to the very question of possession — the material particular. The statement of possession in P's affidavit was knowingly false or at least knowingly misleading. The injunction was ex parte. Therefore the court "shall vacate" it. Add the independent grounds: no reasons recorded under the Rule 3 proviso; no compliance affidavit under proviso (b); no disposal within thirty days under Rule 3A.
- **Shield (plaintiff):** The only answer is that the omission was not knowing and not material — a difficult argument where the suppressed order decides the very issue. P's realistic course is to disclose immediately, apologise, and invite the court to consider, under the exception in the proviso, whether the interests of justice nevertheless require the injunction to stand.

**Illustration 4 — the bank guarantee.** D, a contractor, has furnished an unconditional bank guarantee. The employer invokes it. D sues for an injunction restraining encashment, alleging that the underlying contract was breached by the employer.

- **Sword (contractor):** The only two recognised exceptions are **egregious fraud** of which the bank has notice, going to the root of the transaction, and **irretrievable injustice** or special equities. D must plead one of them with particulars under Order VI Rule 4. A pleaded dispute about performance of the underlying contract is not enough and will be dismissed at the threshold.
- **Shield (employer):** The guarantee is an independent contract between the bank and the beneficiary. The courts do not interfere with the autonomy of that contract; the contractor's remedy lies in damages, or in arbitration under the underlying contract. There is no irreparable injury, because money is by definition compensable.

**Illustration 5 — the injunction against a tenant's eviction.** P, a tenant, sues his landlord D for a permanent injunction restraining dispossession otherwise than in due process of law, and seeks a temporary injunction.

- **Sword (tenant):** Even a tenant whose tenancy has been terminated is entitled to be evicted only in due process of law; a settled possession, however precarious, is protected against forcible dispossession. The prima facie case is possession, not title. Irreparable injury: dispossession from a business premises destroys goodwill.
- **Shield (landlord):** Contend that the civil court's jurisdiction is barred by the applicable Rent Control Act, which provides a complete code and an exclusive forum — a **Section 9 CPC** objection which, if good, disposes of the injunction application without reaching the three principles. This is the correct order of argument: jurisdiction first, then Section 41 of the Specific Relief Act, then the three principles.

**Illustration 6 — mandatory injunction at the interlocutory stage.** P's water connection is severed by D. P seeks an interim **mandatory** injunction directing restoration.

- **Sword (plaintiff):** An interim mandatory injunction is available, but only in a clear case: the court must be satisfied of a **higher standard** than for a prohibitory injunction — a strong prima facie case, and that the injury is such that compensation cannot be an adequate remedy, and that the balance of convenience is clearly in favour. The classic case is restoration of the status quo ante where the defendant has acted stealthily or in defiance of an order.
- **Shield (defendant):** The relief sought at the interlocutory stage is the very relief claimed in the suit; granting it decrees the suit without trial. Courts are, and should be, reluctant.

### 4. Procedural Interlocking Matrix

| Order XXXIX provision | Interlocks with | What the connection does |
|---|---|---|
| RR. 1, 2 | **Ss. 36–42, Specific Relief Act, 1963** | Order XXXIX supplies the procedure; the Specific Relief Act supplies the substantive law of injunctions, including the s. 41 bars. Every injunction application must clear s. 41 first. |
| RR. 1, 2 | S. 94(c) CPC | Section 94 is the source of the power ("supplemental proceedings"); Order XXXIX is its detailed working out. |
| RR. 1, 2 | S. 151 CPC | Where the case falls outside Rules 1 and 2 but justice requires an order, the court may act under s. 151. Hence the invariable heading "Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 read with Section 151". |
| R. 1(a) — alienation | **S. 52, Transfer of Property Act, 1882** (*lis pendens*) | A transfer pendente lite is not void, but binds the transferee to the result. The defendant's best answer to a restraint on alienation is that s. 52 already protects the plaintiff. |
| R. 1(b) — disposal to defraud creditors | **O. XXXVIII R. 5** (attachment before judgment) | Where the claim is for money, Order XXXVIII Rule 5 is the correct remedy, not Order XXXIX. Rule 1(b) is narrow. |
| R. 2 — breach of contract | Ss. 14, 41(e), Specific Relief Act, 1963 | An injunction will not be granted to enforce a contract which cannot be specifically enforced. |
| R. 2A — disobedience | S. 94(c); O. XXI R. 32; Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 | Rule 2A is the primary remedy for breach of a civil court's injunction. Contempt jurisdiction is supplementary, and the High Court is not to be moved where Rule 2A is available. |
| R. 3 proviso | O. V (service) | The applicant's obligation to serve is independent of the court's process. |
| R. 3A | Art. 227, Constitution | Where a court sits on an ex parte injunction beyond thirty days without reasons, the aggrieved party's remedy is a petition under Article 227. |
| R. 4 | **O. XLIII R. 1(r)** | An order under Rules 1, 2, 2A, 4 or 10 is **appealable** under Order XLIII Rule 1(r). This is one of the few interlocutory orders with a statutory appeal. |
| RR. 6, 7 | O. XXVI R. 9 (commissions) | Interim sale of perishable property; detention, preservation and inspection; entry upon land. Usually implemented through a Commissioner. |
| R. 10 | O. XX; deposit of money | Where money is admitted to be held on behalf of another, the court may order deposit. A useful and underused provision. |
| Undertaking as to damages | S. 95 CPC | Where an injunction was obtained on insufficient grounds, or the suit fails and there was no reasonable ground for instituting it, the defendant may apply under **s. 95** for compensation, up to the pecuniary limits of the court's jurisdiction. |

**Appeal and challenge routes**

| Order | Route |
|---|---|
| Grant, refusal, variation or vacation of a temporary injunction (RR. 1, 2, 2A, 4, 10) | **Appeal** under O. XLIII R. 1(r) |
| Order in appeal under O. XLIII | Second appeal is **not** available from an order; the route is Art. 227 or, in a fit case, Art. 136 |
| Ad interim order pending disposal of the application | Ordinarily not appealed; the correct course is to seek early disposal under R. 3A and, failing that, Art. 227 |
| Order of a court refusing to record reasons under the R. 3 proviso | Appeal under O. XLIII R. 1(r), taking the defect as a ground |

**Applications actually filed alongside**

| Application | Provision | Purpose |

| IA under O. XXVI R. 9 | Commission | Local inspection to fix the physical position |

::: warning Court fee and the injunction application
An application under Order XXXIX bears the fixed court fee prescribed by the applicable State Court Fees Act for an interlocutory application; it is not valued ad valorem. But the **suit** for a permanent injunction is valued under the Suits Valuation Act and the State Court Fees Act, and undervaluation of the suit for injunction is a standard threshold objection which, if successful, takes the interlocutory application with it.
:::

### 5. Landmark & Modern Jurisprudence

**Dalpat Kumar v. Prahlad Singh**, (1992) 1 SCC 719 — Supreme Court of India.

*Facts:* The scope of the discretion under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 fell for consideration.

**Ratio decidendi:** The three requirements are cumulative. A **prima facie case** means a serious question to be tried — not that the plaintiff must show he will succeed. **Balance of convenience** requires the court to compare the mischief or injury likely to be caused to the plaintiff by refusing the injunction with that likely to be caused to the defendant by granting it. **Irreparable injury** means an injury which cannot be adequately compensated in damages; it does not mean that there must be no physical possibility of repairing the injury. The satisfaction that there is a prima facie case does not by itself entitle a party to an injunction.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"A prima facie case alone does not entitle a party to a temporary injunction. The court must further be satisfied that the balance of convenience is in the applicant's favour and that refusal would cause an injury which cannot be compensated in money."
:::

---

**Gujarat Bottling Co. Ltd. v. Coca Cola Co.**, (1995) 5 SCC 545 — Supreme Court of India.

*Facts:* An injunction was sought to enforce a negative covenant in a bottling agreement.

**Ratio decidendi:** The grant of an interlocutory injunction is a discretionary and equitable relief, and the court must consider the **conduct of the party** invoking it. He who seeks equity must do equity. A party in breach of his own obligations under the agreement, or who has approached the court with unclean hands or after undue delay, may be refused relief notwithstanding the three principles. The purpose of an interlocutory injunction is to preserve the status quo until the rights of the parties are determined.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"An interlocutory injunction is an equitable remedy, and the conduct of the applicant is a relevant and often decisive consideration. Delay, suppression, and breach of the applicant's own obligations each disentitle him."
:::

---

**Morgan Stanley Mutual Fund v. Kartick Das**, (1994) 4 SCC 225 — Supreme Court of India.

*Facts:* An ex parte injunction was granted restraining a public issue.

**Ratio decidendi:** The court laid down the factors governing the grant of an **ex parte** injunction: whether irreparable or serious mischief will ensue; whether refusal would involve greater injustice than granting it; the time at which the plaintiff first had notice of the act complained of, so that the making of an improper order against a party in his absence is prevented; whether the plaintiff had acquiesced; the general principle that the court will consider the balance of convenience; and, critically, that the applicant must come with **clean hands and full disclosure**. An ex parte injunction should be granted only under exceptional circumstances, and the court must record reasons.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"An ex parte injunction is to be granted only in exceptional circumstances, on recorded reasons, and only where the applicant has made full and frank disclosure of all material facts including those adverse to him."
:::

---

**Shiv Kumar Chadha v. Municipal Corporation of Delhi**, (1993) 3 SCC 161 — Supreme Court of India, three-Judge Bench.

*Facts:* Ex parte injunctions had been granted against demolition action without compliance with the requirements of Rule 3.

**Ratio decidendi:** The proviso to Rule 3 requiring the court to record reasons for granting an injunction without notice is **mandatory**. The requirement is not a formality; the court must apply its mind to why delay would defeat the object, and must say so. Further, Rule 3A obliges the court to dispose of the application within thirty days; where it does not, it must record its reasons. Non-compliance with these requirements is a serious matter, and a court is expected to give priority to such applications.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"The requirement under the proviso to Order XXXIX Rule 3 that the court record its reasons for dispensing with notice is mandatory; an ex parte injunction granted without recorded reasons is unsustainable on that ground alone."
:::

---

**Wander Ltd. v. Antox India (P) Ltd.**, 1990 Supp SCC 727 — Supreme Court of India.

*Facts:* An appeal against the refusal of an interlocutory injunction in a passing-off action.

**Ratio decidendi:** In an appeal against the exercise of discretion at the interlocutory stage, the appellate court will **not** interfere merely because it would have reached a different conclusion. It interferes only where the discretion has been exercised **arbitrarily, capriciously, perversely, or in ignorance of settled principles**, or where the court has ignored the settled principles of law regulating the grant or refusal of interlocutory injunctions. The appellate court does not reassess the material and substitute its own view.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"An appellate court under Order XLIII Rule 1(r) does not re-exercise the discretion. It asks only whether the discretion was exercised reasonably, judicially and on settled principles."
:::

---

**Best Sellers Retail (India) (P) Ltd. v. Aditya Birla Nuvo Ltd.**, (2012) 6 SCC 792 — Supreme Court of India.

*Facts:* An injunction was granted in a distributorship dispute.

**Ratio decidendi:** All three conditions must be satisfied; the presence of a prima facie case is not a substitute for the other two. Where the injury complained of is capable of being compensated in money, an injunction ought not to be granted. The court also reiterated that a temporary injunction cannot be granted where its effect would be to grant, at the interlocutory stage, the very relief claimed in the suit, unless the case is exceptional.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"Where the claim sounds in damages and the defendant is able to pay, there is no irreparable injury and the temporary injunction must be refused, however strong the prima facie case."
:::

---

**Kanwar Singh Saini v. High Court of Delhi**, (2012) 4 SCC 307 — Supreme Court of India.

*Facts:* Proceedings for contempt were initiated in respect of the breach of a civil court's injunction, in circumstances where Order XXXIX Rule 2A was available.

**Ratio decidendi:** Where a decree or an order of injunction of a civil court is disobeyed, the remedy lies under **Order XXXIX Rule 2A** (for an order of temporary injunction) or **Order XXI Rule 32** (for a decree of permanent injunction), and not by way of contempt proceedings before the High Court. The specific remedy provided by the Code must be pursued; the contempt jurisdiction is not to be invoked as a substitute for it. Further, Rule 2A applies only to the breach of an **existing, subsisting** order of injunction — once the suit is disposed of, Rule 2A has no application.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"Disobedience of a temporary injunction is to be punished under Order XXXIX Rule 2A by the court that granted it, not by contempt proceedings in the High Court; and Rule 2A operates only while the injunction subsists."
:::

---

**Ramrameshwari Devi v. Nirmala Devi**, (2011) 8 SCC 249 — Supreme Court of India.

*Facts:* The Court considered the abuse of interlocutory processes to prolong litigation.

**Ratio decidendi:** Courts must be cautious in granting ex parte and interim orders, and must impose **realistic and actual costs** on parties who obtain orders by suppression or who prolong proceedings. The Court emphasised that pleadings and affidavits must be scrutinised, that false averments should attract consequences, and that the practice of granting routine ad interim orders which then subsist for years is to be discouraged.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"An ex parte order obtained on incomplete or false disclosure should attract not merely vacation but actual costs; the court is entitled to compensate the party wrongly restrained."
:::

Every citation, bench strength and current status above must be confirmed against the official report and a current citator before it is placed before a court. The law on injunctions restraining encashment of bank guarantees, and on interim mandatory injunctions, has continued to develop, and the most recent decision on the specific point should be checked.

### 6. The Advocate's Toolkit

**Drafting the injunction application — the anatomy**

| Paragraph | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Description of the parties and the suit property, cross-referenced to the Schedule to the plaint |
| 2–5 | The plaintiff's right: title, possession, contract, or statutory entitlement, with documents |
| 6–8 | The threatened act: **what** the defendant is doing or threatening, **when** it began, and **how** the plaintiff learnt of it. Dates matter — a vague "recently" invites a laches objection |
| 9 | **Prima facie case**, stated as such, referable to the documents annexed |
| 10 | **Balance of convenience**, stated comparatively — not "the balance lies in favour of the plaintiff", but *why* |
| 11 | **Irreparable injury**, explaining specifically why money is not an adequate remedy |
| 12 | **Urgency**, if ex parte relief is sought, with the facts that make notice self-defeating |
| 13 | **Full disclosure** — every prior proceeding, notice, order and adverse fact. State them; do not wait to be caught |
| 14 | Undertaking to abide by any order for damages, and, if appropriate, an offer of security |
| Prayer | Ad interim relief, then final relief on the application, precisely described |

::: danger The eight ways an injunction application is lost
1. **Damages are adequate.** If the claim can be quantified and the defendant can pay, there is no irreparable injury. Consider Order XXXVIII Rule 5 instead.
2. **Section 41, Specific Relief Act.** Check every clause before drafting. A suit to restrain judicial proceedings, to enforce a personal-service contract, or where an equally efficacious remedy exists, is barred at the threshold.
3. **Delay.** An applicant who waited nine months while the construction rose has no equity. If there is delay, explain it in the affidavit; do not leave it to be discovered.
4. **Suppression.** The first proviso to Rule 4 mandates vacation. Disclose the adverse order, the earlier suit, the notice, the criminal proceeding.
5. **Vague prayer.** "Status quo" without specifying as to what and as of when produces an unenforceable order. So does an injunction "restraining the defendants from interfering", which cannot be enforced under Rule 2A because no one can say what was breached.
6. **Property not in dispute in the suit.** Rule 1 does not reach property outside the subject-matter.
7. **No prima facie case because the plaint is defective.** In a specific performance suit, an unpleaded readiness and willingness destroys the injunction with the plaint.
8. **Interim relief identical to final relief.** Asking at the interlocutory stage for the decree itself invites refusal, unless the case is exceptional.
:::

**Precision in the prayer — the difference between an enforceable and an unenforceable order**

| Weak (unenforceable) | Strong (enforceable under Rule 2A) |
|---|---|
| "Restraining the Defendants from interfering with the Plaintiff's rights" | "Restraining the Defendants, their servants, agents and assigns from entering upon the suit property described in Schedule A, or from obstructing the Plaintiff's access thereto through the passage marked X–Y on the site plan" |
| "Status quo be maintained" | "The Defendants be restrained from raising any construction on the suit property beyond the plinth level existing as on the date of this order" |
| "Restraining the Defendants from alienating the property" | "Restraining the Defendants from selling, transferring, mortgaging, gifting, leasing, or otherwise creating any third-party right, title or interest in the suit property described in Schedule A" |
| "Restraining dispossession" | "Restraining the Defendants from dispossessing the Plaintiff from the suit property otherwise than in due process of law" |

**Model prayer — application under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2**

> It is therefore most respectfully prayed that this Hon'ble Court may be pleased to:
>
> **(a)** Grant an ad interim ex parte temporary injunction restraining the Defendants, their servants, agents, employees and all persons claiming through or under them, from selling, transferring, alienating, mortgaging, or otherwise creating any third-party right, title or interest in respect of the suit property more particularly described in Schedule A hereto, pending the hearing and final disposal of the suit;
>
> **(b)** Grant an ad interim ex parte temporary injunction restraining the Defendants, their servants and agents, from raising any construction upon, or altering the nature and character of, the suit property beyond the position existing as on the date of this application;
>
> **(c)** Appoint a Local Commissioner under Order XXVI Rule 9 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 to visit the suit property and to submit a report as to its physical condition, the nature and extent of construction thereon, and the person in actual physical possession;
>
> **(d)** Confirm the ad interim orders aforesaid after notice to the Defendants and until the disposal of the suit;
>
> **(e)** Award the costs of this application to the Plaintiff;
>
> **(f)** Pass such other and further orders as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit and proper in the facts and circumstances of the case, and in the interests of justice.

**Model paragraph — the disclosure paragraph that saves the order**

> **Disclosure:** The Plaintiff states, in discharge of his duty of full and frank disclosure, that (i) a notice dated ____ was received by the Plaintiff from the Defendant, a copy of which is annexed as Annexure P-7; (ii) proceedings under Section ____ are pending between the parties before the ____ Court, being Case No. ____, in which an order dated ____ was passed, a copy of which is annexed as Annexure P-8; and (iii) the Plaintiff has not instituted any other proceeding in respect of the subject matter of this suit before any court or tribunal.

**Strategic timing**

| Objective | When to move |
|---|---|
| Ex parte injunction | With the plaint, on the day of institution. Urgency evaporates with every week of delay, and the delay itself becomes the defendant's answer. |
| Commissioner under O. XXVI R. 9 | Same day. The physical position on the date of institution is the fact you will most want at trial. |
| Vacation under R. 4 | Immediately on service. Take the Rule 3 proviso defects first (no recorded reasons, no compliance affidavit), then suppression under the first proviso, then the merits. |
| Rule 2A for breach | Promptly, and while the injunction subsists. Rule 2A does not survive the disposal of the suit. |
| Section 95 compensation | On dismissal of the suit or vacation of the injunction, and within the pecuniary limits of the court. |
| Appeal under O. XLIII R. 1(r) | Within 30 days (Art. 116, Limitation Act, 1963, for an appeal to a High Court from an order; check the applicable Article and forum). |

## Rule 2A — the consequence of disobedience

### 1. Verbatim Bare Act Text & Statutory Mechanics

> **Rule 2A. Consequence of disobedience or breach of injunction.** — (1) In the case of disobedience of any injunction granted or other order made under rule 1 or rule 2 or breach of any of the terms on which the injunction was granted or the order made, the Court granting the injunction or making the order, or any Court to which the suit or proceeding is transferred, may order the property of the person guilty of such disobedience or breach to be attached, and may also order such person to be detained in the civil prison for a term not exceeding three months, unless in the meantime the Court directs his release.
>
> (2) No attachment made under this rule shall remain in force for more than one year, at the end of which time, if the disobedience or breach continues, the property attached may be sold and out of the proceeds, the Court may award such compensation as it thinks fit to the injured party and shall pay the balance, if any, to the party entitled thereto.

**Statutory mechanics**

| Operative expression | Character | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| "In the case of **disobedience**… or **breach of any of the terms**" | Two limbs | Rule 2A covers both disobedience of the injunction and breach of the conditions on which it was granted (for example, failure to furnish the security ordered). |
| "**The Court granting** the injunction… or any Court to which the suit is transferred" | Forum limitation | The application lies to the court that granted the order. It is not a High Court contempt jurisdiction. |
| "**may** order the property… to be attached" | Discretionary | Attachment is not automatic. The court must be satisfied of wilful disobedience. |
| "**may also** order such person to be **detained in the civil prison** for a term not exceeding **three months**" | Discretionary; capped | Three months is the ceiling. Detention is in the **civil** prison; this is coercive, not penal. |
| "unless in the meantime the Court **directs his release**" | Purgative | The contemnor may purge the disobedience and be released. The object is compliance, not punishment. |
| Sub-rule (2): attachment not to exceed **one year**; thereafter **sale** | Escalation | If disobedience continues for a year, the attached property may be sold and compensation awarded to the injured party from the proceeds. |
| Sub-rule (2): "**shall pay the balance**… to the party entitled" | Mandatory | The surplus returns to the owner. Rule 2A is not confiscatory. |

::: warning The threshold is wilfulness, and the standard of proof is high
Because the consequence is detention, the disobedience must be established to a standard approaching that in criminal contempt: **wilful and deliberate**, not merely a possible or arguable breach. Where the order is ambiguous, or where the alleged contemnor acted on a bona fide interpretation, Rule 2A will not be applied. This is another reason to draft the prayer with precision — an imprecise injunction is unenforceable.
:::

### 2. Plain-English Conceptual Demystification

**Sub-module A — what Rule 2A is for.** It is a **coercive**, not a punitive, jurisdiction. Its object is to secure obedience to the order, which is why detention ends the moment the court directs release, and why attachment is lifted on compliance. Punishment as such belongs to the contempt jurisdiction.

**Sub-module B — the elements the applicant must establish.**

1. A **valid, subsisting** order of injunction under Rule 1 or Rule 2. If the suit has been disposed of, or the injunction has been vacated or has expired, Rule 2A has no application.
2. **Knowledge** of the order on the part of the alleged contemnor. Service, or actual knowledge — an injunction cannot be disobeyed by a person who does not know of it. Rule 5 extends the binding effect to the officers of a corporation.
3. **Disobedience or breach**, identified precisely by reference to the terms of the order.
4. **Wilfulness** — deliberate, conscious violation, not inadvertence and not a bona fide misreading of an ambiguous order.

**Sub-module C — Rule 2A distinguished from its neighbours.**

| | **O. XXXIX R. 2A** | **O. XXI R. 32** | **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applies to | Breach of a **temporary** injunction | Breach of a **decree** for injunction or specific performance | Wilful disobedience of any order of a court |
| Forum | The court that granted the injunction | The executing court | High Court / Supreme Court |
| Consequence | Attachment; civil prison up to **3 months**; sale after 1 year | Attachment; detention; the decree may be executed by the court's own agency | Civil contempt: simple imprisonment up to 6 months, or fine, or both |
| Nature | Coercive | Executory | Punitive and coercive |
| Availability | Only while the injunction subsists | After decree | Not to be invoked where R. 2A or O. XXI R. 32 provides the remedy |

**Sub-module D — the practical problem: restoring the position.**

Rule 2A punishes and coerces, but it does not in terms empower the court to undo what has been done. Where a defendant, in breach of an injunction, has raised a construction or alienated the property, the practical remedy is a combination of:

- **Rule 2A** — attachment and detention, to coerce;
- **Section 151** — a direction to restore the position as it stood, on the principle that no party should benefit from his own disobedience, and that the court can undo what was done in violation of its order;
- **Section 52, Transfer of Property Act, 1882** — where the breach is an alienation, the transferee takes subject to the result of the suit in any event;
- an application to **amend** the plaint to add a prayer for mandatory injunction and demolition.

### 3. Courtroom & Practical Illustrations

**Illustration 1 — construction raised in defiance.** An injunction restrains D from raising construction beyond plinth level. D completes two floors within six weeks.

- **Sword (plaintiff):** Application under Rule 2A, annexing (i) the order, (ii) proof of service or knowledge, (iii) photographs with dates, and (iv) an application under Order XXVI Rule 9 for a Commissioner to record the present state of construction and compare it with the state recorded earlier. Simultaneously, an application under s. 151 for a direction to demolish the offending construction and restore the position as on the date of the order. The two applications together are far stronger than either alone.
- **Shield (defendant):** Attack the precision of the order — was "plinth level" defined? Attack knowledge — when exactly was the order served? Attack wilfulness — was the construction carried out by a contractor under a pre-existing contract, without D's knowledge of the order? Each of these, if established, defeats Rule 2A, because the standard is wilful disobedience.

**Illustration 2 — alienation in breach.** An injunction restrains D from alienating the suit property. D executes a registered sale deed in favour of X.

- **Sword (plaintiff):** Rule 2A against D. As against X, the plaintiff does not need Rule 2A at all: **s. 52 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882** binds X to the result of the suit. Implead X under Order I Rule 10(2) so that the decree binds him directly and is executable against him.
- **Shield (defendant):** Where the agreement to sell predated the injunction and the sale deed merely completed a pre-existing obligation, argue absence of wilfulness — although this is a weak argument if the injunction was clear, since the correct course was to apply for modification, not to proceed.

**Illustration 3 — breach of a condition, not of the injunction.** An injunction is granted on the condition that P furnishes security of ₹20,00,000 within four weeks. P does not.

- **Sword (defendant):** Rule 2A's second limb — "breach of any of the terms on which the injunction was granted". But the more direct and effective remedy is an application under **Rule 4** to vacate, on the ground that the condition on which the order was made has not been satisfied, so that the order has spent itself.
- **Shield (plaintiff):** Apply for extension of time and deposit immediately; courts ordinarily accept late compliance where the delay is short and explained.

### 4. Procedural Interlocking Matrix

| Provision | Interlocks with | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| R. 2A | O. XXXIX RR. 1, 2 | Only breaches of orders under Rules 1 and 2 are covered |
| R. 2A | R. 5 | Extends the reach of the injunction, and therefore of Rule 2A, to the officers of a corporate defendant |

### 5. Landmark & Modern Jurisprudence

**Food Corporation of India v. Sukh Deo Prasad**, (2009) 5 SCC 665 — Supreme Court of India.

*Facts:* Proceedings under Order XXXIX Rule 2A were taken in respect of an order which, on analysis, was not an order of injunction under Rules 1 or 2.

**Ratio decidendi:** The power under Rule 2A is **restricted to cases of disobedience or breach of an injunction granted or order made under Rules 1 and 2**. It does not extend to disobedience of orders made under other provisions, nor to orders of a general nature. Further, since the consequence is detention in civil prison, the provision must be **strictly construed**, and the disobedience must be established as wilful; the standard is akin to that in a contempt proceeding.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"Order XXXIX Rule 2A applies only to the breach of an injunction granted under Rules 1 or 2 and must be strictly construed; the disobedience must be wilful and must be proved, not inferred."
:::

---

**Kanwar Singh Saini v. High Court of Delhi**, (2012) 4 SCC 307 — Supreme Court of India.

*Facts:* Contempt was initiated for breach of an injunction where the specific remedy under the Code was available.

**Ratio decidendi:** Where the Code provides a specific remedy — Rule 2A for a temporary injunction, Order XXI Rule 32 for a decree — that remedy must be pursued, and the contempt jurisdiction is not to be used as a substitute. Rule 2A applies only during the pendency of the suit and while the injunction subsists; once the suit is decided, an injunction under Rule 1 or 2 comes to an end and Rule 2A ceases to be available.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"Rule 2A operates only while the temporary injunction subsists; after decree the remedy for breach of a permanent injunction is execution under Order XXI Rule 32."
:::

---

**Samee Khan v. Bindu Khan**, (1998) 7 SCC 59 — Supreme Court of India.

*Facts:* The relationship between the two limbs of Rule 2A — attachment and detention — was in question.

**Ratio decidendi:** The two consequences under Rule 2A(1) are **independent**. The court may order attachment of property, or detention in civil prison, or both. It is not necessary that attachment must precede detention, nor that the two must go together. The provision confers a discretion as to which consequence, or both, is appropriate to secure obedience.

::: tip Practitioner Takeaway
"Attachment and detention under Rule 2A are alternative and cumulative remedies; the court may order either or both, and detention is not conditional upon a prior attachment."
:::

::: info Verify before citing
Citations, bench strength and current status must be verified against the official report and a current citator before reliance. The interaction between Rule 2A and the contempt jurisdiction has been considered in several later decisions and should be checked for the position in the relevant High Court.
:::

### 6. The Advocate's Toolkit

**Proving a Rule 2A breach — the evidentiary package**

| Element | Proof |
|---|---|
| The order | Certified copy of the order, and of the application on which it was made |
| Knowledge | Affidavit of service; postal receipts and tracking; the compliance affidavit filed under the Rule 3 proviso; or the contemnor's appearance in court on the date of the order |
| The breach | Local Commissioner's report under Order XXVI Rule 9 (the strongest); dated photographs; municipal records; the registered instrument, if the breach is an alienation |
| Wilfulness | The interval between service and breach; correspondence; the absence of any application for modification |

::: danger Why Rule 2A applications fail
1. **The order was imprecise.** "Status quo" and "do not interfere" cannot be enforced, because no one can say exactly what was prohibited. Draft the prayer for enforceability.
2. **Knowledge is not proved.** Get the compliance affidavit on record under the Rule 3 proviso; it is the best evidence of service.
3. **The suit has been disposed of.** Rule 2A dies with the injunction. After decree, use Order XXI Rule 32.
4. **The breach is arguable.** Where the defendant's conduct is consistent with a bona fide reading of the order, wilfulness fails.
5. **The wrong forum.** Rule 2A lies to the court that granted the order, not to the High Court in contempt.
6. **No restorative prayer.** Rule 2A coerces but does not undo. Add a Section 151 prayer for restoration in the same application.
:::

**Model prayer — application under Order XXXIX Rule 2A**

> It is therefore most respectfully prayed that this Hon'ble Court may be pleased to:
>
> **(a)** Hold that the Defendants have wilfully disobeyed the order of injunction dated ____ passed by this Hon'ble Court in IA No. ____ of 20__;
>
> **(b)** Order the attachment of the properties of the Defendants described in Schedule B hereto under Order XXXIX Rule 2A of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908;
>
> **(c)** Order the detention of Defendant No. 1 in civil prison for such term, not exceeding three months, as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit;
>
> **(d)** In exercise of the inherent powers of this Hon'ble Court under Section 151 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, direct the Defendants to restore the suit property to the position in which it stood on ____, being the date of the said order, and in default, to authorise the Plaintiff to carry out such restoration at the cost of the Defendants;
>
> **(e)** Appoint a Local Commissioner under Order XXVI Rule 9 to inspect the suit property and report upon the present state thereof;
>
> **(f)** Award the costs of this application to the Plaintiff;
>
> **(g)** Pass such other and further orders as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit in the interests of justice.

## Rules 6 to 10 — the other interlocutory orders

These rules are short, underused, and occasionally decisive.

| Rule | Text in substance | When it is worth using |
|---|---|---|
| **R. 6 — Interim sale** | The court may order the sale of any movable property which is the subject-matter of the suit, or attached before judgment, which is subject to speedy and natural decay, or which for any other just and sufficient reason it may be desirable to have sold at once. | Perishable goods; goods whose value is falling; a vessel or vehicle deteriorating in custody. The sale proceeds stand in place of the goods. |
| **R. 7 — Detention, preservation, inspection** | The court may, on the application of any party, order the detention, preservation or inspection of any property which is the subject-matter of the suit; authorise entry upon or into any land or building in the possession of any other party; and authorise samples to be taken, observations to be made or experiments to be tried. | The everyday companion of the injunction application. Entry, inspection and sampling — invaluable in construction, manufacturing and IP disputes. |
| **R. 8** | Application by the plaintiff for an order under R. 6 or R. 7 may be made after notice to the defendant at any time after institution of the suit; application by the defendant, at any time after appearance. | Fixes the timing. |
| **R. 9 — Property in dispute in the possession of a third party** | Where land paying revenue, or a tenure, is the subject of the suit and is liable to sale for arrears, any party may pay the arrears and be charged upon the property. | Rare, but decisive where a revenue sale would extinguish the subject-matter. |
| **R. 10 — Deposit of money in court** | Where the subject-matter of a suit is money or some other thing capable of delivery, and any party admits that he holds it as a trustee for another party, or that it belongs to another party, the court may order it to be deposited in court or delivered, on such terms as to security or otherwise as the court thinks fit. | Where the defendant admits holding the money but disputes the entitlement. Converts a contested sum into a deposit and neutralises the risk of dissipation. |

::: tip Rule 7 is the most underused rule in the Order
In a construction dispute, an application under Rule 7 for inspection and for permission to take samples, coupled with a Commissioner under Order XXVI Rule 9, produces a contemporaneous, court-authenticated record of the physical position. That record is worth more at trial than any number of affidavits, and it is available for the asking on notice.
:::

## Chapter consolidation — the interlocutory stage in one view

**The order of argument in an injunction application**

| Step | Question | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the civil court have jurisdiction at all? | S. 9 CPC; the special statute's ouster clause |
| 2 | Is the injunction barred? | **S. 41, Specific Relief Act, 1963** |
| 3 | Is there a prima facie case? | Judge-made; *Dalpat Kumar* |
| 4 | Where does the balance of convenience lie? | Judge-made; *Dalpat Kumar* |
| 5 | Is the injury irreparable — that is, non-compensable in money? | Judge-made; *Best Sellers Retail* |
| 6 | Does the applicant's conduct disentitle him — delay, suppression, unclean hands? | *Gujarat Bottling*; *Morgan Stanley* |
| 7 | If ex parte: are the Rule 3 conditions met — recorded reasons, immediate service, compliance affidavit? | R. 3 proviso; *Shiv Kumar Chadha* |
| 8 | What are the terms — security, undertaking, account, duration? | R. 2(2) |

**The defendant's checklist on receiving an ex parte injunction**

1. Obtain a certified copy of the order **and** of the application and affidavit. Check whether the copies were served as required by the Rule 3 proviso (a).
2. Check whether the **compliance affidavit** under proviso (b) was filed on the day of the order or the next day. If not, that is a ground.
3. Check whether the court **recorded reasons** for dispensing with notice. If not, *Shiv Kumar Chadha* applies.
4. Check whether **thirty days** have elapsed without disposal and without recorded reasons — Rule 3A.
5. Identify every material fact **suppressed** by the applicant. If any is knowingly false or misleading and material, the first proviso to Rule 4 mandates vacation.
6. Only then, argue the three principles and Section 41 of the Specific Relief Act.
7. File the application under **Rule 4**, and consider an appeal under **Order XLIII Rule 1(r)** if the order is confirmed.
8. Preserve the claim for **compensation under Section 95** if the injunction was obtained on insufficient grounds.

::: tip The four sentences worth memorising from this chapter
1. **"Three principles, all three."** Prima facie case, balance of convenience, irreparable injury — cumulative, not alternative. A strong prima facie case does not carry an application in which damages are an adequate remedy.
2. **"Section 41 comes before the three principles."** The Specific Relief Act tells you whether an injunction can be granted at all; Order XXXIX tells you how.
3. **"An ex parte order without recorded reasons is bad on its face."** The proviso to Rule 3 is mandatory, and non-compliance is a ground for vacation independent of the merits.
4. **"Draft the prayer so that a breach is provable."** An injunction you cannot enforce under Rule 2A is not worth obtaining.
:::

::: warning Before you file
Order XXXIX must be read with Sections 36 to 42 of the Specific Relief Act, 1963, with Section 94 and Section 151 of the Code, and with the applicable State amendments and High Court Rules, several of which regulate the practice on ex parte injunctions and the time within which they must be listed. Verify the current text of the Rules as amended in your State, and the current status of every judgment, before filing.
:::
